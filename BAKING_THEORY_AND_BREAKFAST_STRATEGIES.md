## The Core Formula of Baking

### Universal Formula 🎯

```
Base ingredients: Flour + Liquid + Seasoning (sugar, salt, oil)

+ Nothing          → Dense/unleavened (flatbread, dumpling skin, noodles)
+ Baking powder    → Soft & crumbly (pancakes, cakes)
+ Yeast            → Soft & elastic (bread, buns)
+ Whipped egg white → Light & delicate (chiffon cake)
```

### Essential Understanding

**Baking is a "trade" - exchanging ingredients for air pockets:**

```
Yeast fermentation:
Input: Sugar + Time
Output: CO2 + Bread aroma + Elasticity

Baking powder:
Input: Chemical substance
Output: CO2 (no aroma)

Whipped egg whites:
Input: Effort + Time
Output: Air bubbles (fine & delicate)
```

## Four Ways to Make Things Fluffy

### 1️⃣ Nothing Added (Unleavened)

```
Characteristics:
❌ No air pockets
✅ Dense, chewy
✅ Can be shaped

Good for:
🥟 Dumpling skins
🍜 Noodles
🫓 Flatbreads, crepes
🥠 Spring roll wrappers
```

### 2️⃣ Baking Powder (Chemical Leavening)

```
Principle:
Baking powder + Water + Heat → Instant CO2 production

Characteristics:
💨 Large, rough air pockets
😐 No fermentation aroma
⚡ Fast! No waiting needed
🧁 Crumbly, cake-like texture

Good for:
🥞 Pancakes, waffles
🧁 Muffins, cupcakes
🍪 Some cookies
🫓 Quick breads
```

### 3️⃣ Yeast (Biological Leavening)

```
Principle:
Yeast + Sugar + Time → Slow CO2 production + Aroma

Characteristics:
💨 Small, uniform air pockets
🍞 Bread aroma
⏰ Requires fermentation time
💪 Elastic, can stretch

Good for:
🍞 Bread, toast
🥐 Steamed buns, baozi
🫓 Flower rolls
🥖 Baguettes
```

### 4️⃣ Whipped Egg Whites (Physical Aeration)

```
Principle:
Beating egg whites → Air trapped by protein → Fine bubbles

Characteristics:
💨 Very fine air pockets
🎂 Light, melt-in-mouth texture
🥚 Requires technique (whipping + folding)
🌬️ Fluffiest result

Good for:
🎂 Chiffon cake
🍮 Soufflé
🥞 Japanese-style thick pancakes
🍰 Angel food cake
```

## Folding Technique

### Why Fold Instead of Stir?

**Core question: Won't the bubbles get crushed when mixing whipped egg whites into batter?**

### Answer: Folding ≠ Stirring!

```
❌ Stirring (Wrong):
Motion: Circular stirring
Effect: Crushes bubbles → Bubbles pop → Wasted effort
Analogy: Wringing out a sponge → Water escapes

✅ Folding (Correct):
Motion: Bottom to top (like stir-frying!)
Effect: Gentle integration → Most bubbles preserved
Analogy: Gently turning a sponge → Air pockets remain
```

### Correct Folding Technique

```
1. Cut spatula vertically through center
2. Scrape along bowl bottom
3. Lift from edge upward (like shoveling soil)
4. Rotate bowl 90°
5. Repeat 20-30 times

⚠️ Key points:
- Light movements!
- Don't stir in circles!
- Minimize repetitions!
- Slight unevenness is OK
```

### Why Does This Preserve Bubbles?

```
Whipped egg whites ≠ Bare air bubbles

Whipped egg whites = Air trapped in protein "cages"
                    Like a sponge with structural support

During folding:
- Some bubbles are lost (20-40%)
- But most are preserved (60-80%)
- Bubbles expand during baking
- Enough for fluffy results!

During stirring:
- Bubbles are repeatedly crushed
- 70-90% lost
- Not enough remaining
- Result isn't fluffy
```

## Batter vs Dough

### Why Sometimes Batter, Sometimes Dough?

**Key: Water content determines everything!**

```
Dough = Flour + Less water (40-50%)
        Can be kneaded, doesn't flow
        Good for: Bread, buns, dumplings

Batter = Flour + More water (100-150%)
         Flows like thick yogurt
         Good for: Pancakes, cakes, crepes
```

### Why Use Batter for Pancakes?

```
Dough for pancakes:
Too thick → Can't pour → Press by hand → Becomes unleavened → Hard

Batter for pancakes:
Flows well → Pours into pan → Baking powder creates gas → Fluffy!
```

### Why Use Egg Yolk Batter for Cakes?

```
Processing whole eggs together:
- Whipping results mediocre
- Yolk fat interferes with whipping

Processing separately:
Egg whites whipped alone → Maximum bubbles!
Egg yolks made into batter → Adds color, nutrition
Then fold together → Best results!

This is the secret to chiffon cake!
```

### Within the System (Gas Leavening)

| Food | Principle | Notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| **Cream cake** | Whipping | Cake (whipped egg whites) + Cream (whipped) |
| **Mousse** | Whipping | Whipped cream + Fruit purée + Gelatin |

| **Mille crepe cake** | None | Thin crepes + Whipped cream layered |

### Outside the System (New Principles!)

#### 🥐 Choux Pastry/Puffs (Steam Expansion)

```
Brand new principle: Water becomes steam and inflates the pastry!

Process:
1. Flour + Water + Butter → Cook (scald the flour)
2. Add lots of eggs → Mix into paste
3. Pipe into shapes → Bake at high temp (200°C+)

During baking:
🔥 High heat
💧 Water in batter becomes steam
💨 Steam volume is 1600x water volume!
🎈 Pushes outward from inside
🔒 Outer layer solidifies into shell

Result: Crispy shell outside, hollow inside!
Fill with cream → Cream puffs!
```

#### 🥐 Puff Pastry (Physical Layering + Steam)

```
Principle: Multiple layers of butter + dough

Process:
Wrap butter in dough → Roll flat → Fold → Refrigerate
→ Repeat 6 times (final result: 729 layers!)

During baking:
Butter melts → Creates steam
Steam separates each layer
→ Flaky, layered texture!

Very difficult, professional technique!
```

#### 🥐 Croissants (Combined Techniques)

```
One of the hardest baking challenges!

Principle: Yeast fermentation + Puff pastry technique

Has both yeast's softness
And pastry's flaky layers

Complex process, high skill requirement
```

## No-Knead Bread Temperature Strategy

### Choose Fermentation Method Based on Temperature

```
🌡️ Room temp 10-15°C (50-59°F, Winter):
   Room temperature fermentation 6-8 hours
   No refrigerator needed!

🌡️ Room temp 15-20°C (59-68°F):
   Room temp fermentation 4-6 hours
   Or refrigerate 12 hours

🌡️ Room temp 20-25°C (68-77°F):
   Must refrigerate!
   Room temp will over-ferment → Sour

💡 Key: Poke holes in plastic wrap for ventilation!
   Otherwise oxygen deprived → Sour
```

### Example Time Planning

```
Winter (12°C / 54°F):
Evening 9:00 PM → Mix dough
                → Cover (perforated plastic wrap)
                → Leave at room temperature
Morning 7:00 AM → Fermentation complete (10 hours)
                → Bake directly!

Alternative:
Evening 11:00 PM → Mix dough
                 → Refrigerate
Morning 9:00 AM  → Remove, warm to room temp
                 → Shape → Second rise → Bake
```
